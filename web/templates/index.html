    How a run works

    1. Search like a local. Gaming hashtags and phrases in the market's own language (and Russian for Estonia), YouTube searches in that language with the region set, Shorts search. Each source gets a couple of pages; after that, sources that yield creators get more of the budget.
    2. Hard filters in code. TikTok registration region or YouTube channel country, language and local signals, follower/subscriber band (soft cap: up to 1.5× is kept and flagged), private and inactive accounts. Nothing is discarded; every account keeps its reason.
    3. Free pre-judge. An LLM looks at what we already have for free (bio, a few captions) and only a confident "no" is skipped before we pay for more data.
    4. Enrich. TikTok profile and recent videos; YouTube uploads from the official API, with long videos and Shorts measured separately. Average views over the last 30 days, with the window stated.
    5. Judge fit. Would a young PC-gaming audience watch this creator? Niche, games, risks, and a verbatim evidence quote. No evidence, no accept.
    6. Snowball. Creators follow creators from their own country. Once two are accepted, their following lists are expanded between search pages. This is where most small-market finds come from.

    Public, logged-out data only. The tool drafts; a person decides and sends.
